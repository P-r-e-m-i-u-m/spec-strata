Three kinds of system prompt, and most comparisons mix them up.

I read what Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI have published about how their models should behave, sorted it into three layers, and graded how far each source can be trusted. The paper, the data and my notes are all here.

What I found

Five things that stood out

01

Labs publish different kinds of document

Anthropic's main public document is a training-time one. OpenAI's is a runtime rulebook. xAI publishes the deployed prompts. Google DeepMind hasn't published anything about Gemini's own rules. A "Lab A vs Lab B" comparison often lines up two real things and two gaps.

02

Same structure, different reasons

Anthropic and OpenAI both rank whose instructions come first. OpenAI frames that as developer control. Anthropic frames it as judgment the model should absorb, and says it would rather explain than hand over a rule list.

03

Transparency can be a reaction

xAI committed to publishing Grok's prompts after a May 2025 incident, as a way to rebuild trust. That's a different story from publishing on principle, and it may deserve a different level of trust.

04

Product type shapes the prompt

Coding agents lean on tool rules (what to call, when to confirm, how to format diffs) and say comparatively little about content safety. My reading is that safety already lives in the model. That's an interpretation I haven't tested.

05

"Living document" can be checked, at least once

Anthropic keeps a dated changelog for its claude.ai prompt: 18 revisions in about 23 months, roughly one every six weeks. For the other labs there's nothing to check against.

Still open

What this doesn't settle

→

A second coder

I coded everything myself. A second person scoring the same documents is the quickest way to find out how much of this is judgment.

→

The other labs over time

Only Anthropic has a dated revision record. Whether OpenAI, Google and xAI update their documents as often is unknown, not denied.

→

Why coding agents differ

My explanation is that safety already lives in the model. It fits the data. I haven't tested it against a case that could prove it wrong.

→

Google's silence

I can see that Google hasn't published a governance document. I can't tell whether that's a choice or just a different way of doing things.
